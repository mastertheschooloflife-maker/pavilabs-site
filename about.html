About me

I am Pavisanth Pathmarajah, and PaviLabs grew out of an experience familiar to anyone who has ever prepared for an exam: the methods that actually work are rarely where you look for them. You find them by trying, failing and refusing to stop. At some point I had collected so many that throwing them away was no longer an option. They became books, then apps, then the tools on this site. The name carries the programme: PaviLabs holds my first name, the motto holds the direction. Paving Paths to a smarter future.

The beginning: an exam year as a laboratory

In 2019 I finished my Abitur, the German university entrance exam, among the top graduates of my year with a grade average of 1.1. More interesting than the number is what happened on the way: I treated every exam preparation as an experiment. What actually earns marks, what merely burns time? Which routine survives when motivation fades? The notes from those years became my first book, Englisch meistern, published the same year. Oral exams, French and study methodology itself followed. The books are not theory from the outside; they are the laboratory written up from within.

Eight books, two worlds

Today there are eight published books, in two worlds. The Meistern series for school and the Abitur covers English, French and learning itself. The AI series, in German and English, takes artificial intelligence from buzzword to everyday practice, from a first introduction without prerequisites to a practitioner's handbook covering prompt engineering, agents and automation. The two worlds share one idea: ordering complex material until it becomes usable.

All titles with descriptions and links are under Publications. More books are in the works, from a degree-choice compass to IT security.

From book to app: knowledge in daily doses

Books explain thoroughly, but they do not remind you to keep going. That is what the Daily Dose apps are built for: one short lesson a day, in four languages, with certificates as milestones. Daily Dose of Security turns digital self-defence into a habit, from spotting phishing to password behaviour, and is available on the App Store. Daily Dose of AI applies the same principle to artificial intelligence and has been submitted to both stores. Both apps run without an account and without data collection; what you learn stays with you.

Daily Dose of Security on the App Store. More about both apps is on the home page.

Fourteen tools

The tools on this website are the third piece of the puzzle: flashcard trainers, analysis and writing workshops, an exam coach, a prompt workshop, self-checks for choosing a degree. Everything runs in your browser, with no sign-up, no transmission, no storage. The content comes from the books; the tools make it trainable. Those who want to go deeper reach for the book, but nobody has to.

All fourteen are under Tools. They are in German, built for students preparing for German exams.
